NIOSH Occupational Exposure Banding e-Tool

Preliminary / Initial Exposure AssessmentWorker ExposureChemical hazard

Provisional entry. Compiled from public sources and pending confirmation against the developer's official page. Details and the access link may be refined.

Overview

The NIOSH Occupational Exposure Banding e-Tool operationalizes the NIOSH occupational exposure banding process. For a chemical that has no authoritative occupational exposure limit (OEL), the user enters toxicological information across several health endpoints; the tool compares the data against NIOSH banding criteria and assigns the chemical an overall occupational exposure band (OEB) from A to E — the most protective band among the endpoints with data. Each band corresponds to an order-of-magnitude range of airborne concentrations expected to be protective.

Exposure Pathway(s)

Inhalation (the output bands are airborne-concentration ranges). Skin and eye endpoints are also evaluated as hazard inputs.

Tool Type

Platform
Web application
Output type
Exposure band (A–E)

Use Cases (When to Use / Applications)

Screening-level assessment of chemicals that have no authoritative OEL, to set a target airborne-concentration band for prioritizing and selecting exposure controls. Tier 1 uses GHS-based hazard information; Tier 2 uses point-of-departure data and is intended for a trained occupational hygienist.

Inputs & Outputs

Inputs (What you provide)

Toxicological / hazard data for the chemical across the health endpoints NIOSH evaluates (for example carcinogenicity, reproductive toxicity, specific target-organ toxicity, genotoxicity, respiratory and skin sensitization, acute toxicity, skin corrosion/irritation, and eye damage) — as GHS categories (Tier 1) or point-of-departure data (Tier 2).

Outputs (What you get)

An occupational exposure band from A (highest / least hazardous range) to E (lowest / most hazardous range), taken as the most protective band across the endpoints with data.

Known Limitations / Notes

  • An occupational exposure band is screening guidance, not a regulatory limit; it does not replace an authoritative OEL or compliance with OSHA permissible exposure limits.
  • Accuracy depends on the quality and quantity of the hazard data entered.
  • The tool supports Tier 1 and Tier 2 only; Tier 3 requires expert toxicological judgment outside the tool, and NIOSH advises some substances (e.g., certain nanomaterials) require a Tier 3 assessment.

Authors / Developers

National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health (NIOSH), part of the U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC).
