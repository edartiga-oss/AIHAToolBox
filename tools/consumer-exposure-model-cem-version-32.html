Consumer Exposure Model (CEM) Version 3.2

Product Exposure ModelingProduct AssessmentChemical hazard

Overview

Assesses indoor (or outdoor) exposure to emissions or releases from consumer products and articles. Allows users to enter scenario-specific inputs, or chooser from default exposure scenarios.

Exposure Pathway(s)

Dermal, inhalation and oral

Route Details

There are several options available for simulations based on the route of exposure. For inhalation exposure, a two-zone model is the default, with the option for a zone 1 being divided into a very immediate near field, and a far field within zone 1. For dermal absorption, there is the option for use of a fraction absorbed model, or a permeability model for the scenario of a product applied to the skin.

Inputs & Outputs

Inputs (What you need)

Default scenario, physical/chemical properties, use metrics, averaging time, room volume, receptor activities, weight fraction

Outputs (What you get)

Generates a formatted report with chronic (LADD, LADC), acute (ADR), and peak concentration estimates.

Target Application

Target Population
Consumers
Product Type
Products are broken down into the large categories of products (i.e., formulated consumer products) and articles. CEM is pre-loaded with 53 specific product scenarios, 11 generic product scenarios, 20 specific article scenarios, and one generic article scenario.
Physical Form
Liquid, vapor, and solids

Physical Form Details

Exposure to volatile organic compounds (VOCs) and semi-volatile organic compounds (SVOCs)

Strengths

Relatively easy to use for such a sophistocated model; however, CEM requires a number of parameter inputs though there is also the option to use default values from selected scenario. One would be advised to understand the implications of using all of the default values.

Known Limitations / Notes

CEM is sophisticated and requires a bit of research to use properly. There is a relatively lengthy list of inputs, though default values are available if using one of the existing scenarios. CEM is a higher tier model that should probably be used after utilization of a screening level exposure model. Unfortunately, that may not be apparent to a casual user.
