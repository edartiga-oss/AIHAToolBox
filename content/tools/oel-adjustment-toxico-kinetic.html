Overview

The Toxico-kinetic Extended Shift OEL Adjustment is an AIHA Excel workbook that adjusts a standard 8-hour occupational exposure limit (OEL) for extended or nonstandard work shifts. It applies the toxicokinetic (pharmacokinetic) model of Hickey and Reist (1977), accounting for the contaminant's biological half-life and the worker's schedule — longer exposure periods and shorter recovery periods — so that the adjusted limit preserves the margin of safety assumed by the standard 8-hour limit.

Assessment Phase(s)

Applied to OELs after exposure assessment (limit adjustment)

Exposure Pathway(s)

Inhalation (airborne chemical time-weighted-average OELs)

Tool Type

Platform
Excel workbook
Macro-enabled
No

Use Cases (When to Use / Applications)

Adjusting 8-hour TWA chemical OELs for workers on extended or compressed schedules (for example 10- or 12-hour shifts, or more than 40 hours per week), where increased exposure time and reduced recovery time would otherwise allow body burden to exceed that assumed by the standard limit.

Inputs & Outputs

Inputs (What you provide)

The standard 8-hour TWA OEL; the nonstandard work schedule (hours per day and days per week); and the substance's biological half-life. A separate tab handles chemicals whose half-life is unknown.

Outputs (What you get)

A shift-adjusted (generally reduced) OEL appropriate for the extended or nonstandard schedule.

Known Limitations / Notes

  • Applicable only to chemicals whose limits are based on accumulated systemic body burden — not to limits driven by odor, irritancy, or other acute, non-systemic effects.
  • Requires a biological half-life, which is unavailable for many chemicals (hence the separate "unknown half-life" tab).
  • As a pharmacokinetic method it gives the least conservative adjustment of the common approaches, and half-life estimates can be rough.

Authors / Developers

Published by AIHA. The workbook implements the method of J. L. S. Hickey & P. C. Reist, "Application of Occupational Exposure Limits to Unusual Work Schedules," Am. Ind. Hyg. Assoc. J. 38(11):613–621 (1977), as presented by Armstrong et al.
