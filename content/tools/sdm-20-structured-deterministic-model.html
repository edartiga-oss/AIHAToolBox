Overview

SDM 2.0™ is an Excel-based user-friendly tool for estimating airborne concentrations that represent potential occupational inhalation exposure to volatile and semi-volatile chemicals and chemical mixtures, aerosols, particulates and fibers. It provides a point estimate of the 95th percentile value of an exposure distribution that can be compared to an occupational exposure limit as a measure of health risk for a worker or group of workers.

Assessment Phase(s)

Preliminary Assessment

Exposure Pathway(s)

Inhalation

Route Details

For volatile and semi-volatile chemicals and mixtures, predicts concentration that becomes airborne primarily due to evaporation (i.e., under atmospheric conditions).

Inputs & Outputs

Inputs (What you need)

For Chemical Exposure Evaluation inputs include: Substance or Mixture Components identified by the user, Weight percent of each component (select 100% for pure substance), Vapor Pressure, OEL to be used for the evaluation, Observed Level of Control (from categories provided). For Particulate Exposure Evaluation inputs include: OEL concentration range, Observed Level of Control.

Outputs (What you get)

For Chemical and Particulate Exposures: Health Risk Ranking based on Inputs and Identified Level of Control.

Use Cases (When to Use / Applications)

SDM 2.0™ is typically used when:

  • You need to perform "what-if" comparisons (e.g., ventilation changes, task duration changes, control options)
  • Measurement data are limited, and you need a defensible estimate to support exposure judgments, planning, or prioritization

Strengths

User-friendly dashboard with only a few, easy to follow steps. Provides predicted airborne concentration levels for gases/vapors across a range of engineering controls/ventilation assumptions, to assess impact of controls on potential exposure levels. Can assess exposure of both mixtures and their individual chemical components, and, for chemical mixtures, can determine the controlling compound and provide the fraction of each chemical releative to its OEL. Uses the same Exposure Control Category framework as outlined in the AIHA publication "Assessing and Managing Occupational Exposures (4th Edition)."

Known Limitations / Notes

Not appropriate for assessing scenarios involving thermal decomposition, polymers, or chemicals under pressure. Concentration levels not directly estimated for aerosols, particulates, and fibers.
